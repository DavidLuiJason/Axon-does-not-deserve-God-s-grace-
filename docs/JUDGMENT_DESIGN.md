# AXON JUDGMENT AND FORESIGHT: DESIGN v1 (Director, 2026-10-25; PROPOSED, the owner decides)

[Judgment stage = a part of the brain (roadmap 7 and 7A) that looks at a situation, thinks AHEAD about what each possible action would cause, and says honestly whether the wish will work, work differently, or not work, with how sure it is. Meaning Map = what the reading stages (B03B) hand it: who and what a message is about, in a tree of meanings. J0 to J5 = the small build jobs of this stage. B04A, B06A = brain jobs named in `docs/BRAIN_PLAN.md`.]

Source: the owner's words of 2026-10-25 (the boulder example; "yes is yes, 1% is 1%"; flexible, not fixed; the personality; tools before the brain; guide the user so their own mind shines) and the earlier notes in `docs/AXON_VISION.md`. Nothing here is built. It needs the owner's yes and then its own design check (roadmap T9) per job.

## 1. What this stage is for (the personality)
AXON does not just do what it is told and does not just answer. It THINKS THROUGH what it is about to do, tells the owner what it found, and helps the owner see further. Three promises:
1. **Think ahead.** Before acting, ask "what happens next if I do this, and if I do that instead?", several steps deep, and choose the action whose whole chain ends well, not the action that looks best alone.
2. **Say it straight.** A verdict means what it says: WORKS, WORKS BUT DIFFERENTLY, WORKS ONLY IF (a stated condition), WILL NOT WORK (and why), CANNOT JUDGE (and what is missing). The chance that goes with it is a real number with its basis: 1% is said as 1%, 50% as 50%, 70% as 70%; never rounded up to please or down to scare.
3. **Make the person's own mind shine.** AXON shows its reasoning, offers the owner two or three real options with what each leads to, says when the owner's own idea is the better one, and pushes back kindly with a reason when a plan has a hole. It does not do the owner's thinking unasked.

## 2. The boulder example, as the first known-answer test
A man stands with a small stone on his foot. Around him: a medium boulder and a big boulder, and a wall. If he pushes the small stone LEFT it hits the medium boulder, which hits the wall, rolls back and pushes the big boulder onto him. If he pushes it RIGHT it is clear. A tool that only removes "the thing on the foot" or "the biggest thing" fails. Judgment must (a) hold the SITUATION as items and relations, (b) try each action and follow what it sets off, (c) compare the endings, (d) pick RIGHT and say why, (e) say what it could not foresee. This scenario is test J0-1 and the stage is not accepted until it passes together with the others in section 7.

## 3. How it thinks: a model of the situation, then a look-ahead
**The Situation (small, typed, visible to the owner):**
- ITEMS: things with a size or effort, a state and an owner (a file, a task, a deadline, a phone's free space, a person's time, a stone).
- RELATIONS between items: blocks, needs, hits (pushes), uses the same resource as, is the undo of, belongs to.
- ACTIONS: what AXON (or the owner) can do, each declaring its EFFECTS on items and relations, its cost, whether it can be undone and what it needs first.
- GOAL: what must be true, and by when. RESOURCES: time, space, effort, permission, the AI limits of the day.
**The look-ahead:** from the present state try each allowed action, apply its declared effects, and continue for a few steps (depth and breadth bounded by a budget; the phone's tier sets the budget). Score each ending: goal reached, how soon, what was damaged, what cannot be undone, how many assumptions it rests on. Keep the best few chains and the worst chain of each. Prefer, in this order: safe, reversible, reaches the goal, cheap, soon.
**Where numbers come from:** a chance is computed from the model, never typed in: chains multiply (three steps that each work nine times in ten work 73% of the time, not 90%); pace comes from the journal of real past work; a guess with no data is labelled HYPOTHESIS and shown as a range.
**Calibration:** every verdict with a chance is written to the journal with the result when it is known. AXON reports its own record ("things I called 70% came true 8 times in 11"). A stage whose record drifts is flagged, and its numbers are shown wider until it is fixed. This is how "70% is 70%" is kept honest over time.

## 4. Flexible, not fixed
Skills are small typed functions that read the Situation; they are not lists of rules or phrases (FL13). Which skills run, in what order and with what weights depends on the situation type, the owner's saved preferences (never saved without a yes) and the Charter. Fixed means only what the owner said is fixed: the Charter, the safety rules (a clause with a condition never runs alone; send, delete and money start at ASK or NEVER), the closed grammar sets. Everything else must be able to change tomorrow without rewriting the stage (add a skill, change a weight, add an effect to a tool). When AXON's model has no slot for something, it says "my model of this does not include that" instead of guessing.

## 5. The skills (the owner's three examples plus the rest; each is one small job)
**A. Seeing the situation**
1. Find the REAL blocker: the thing whose removal frees the most, checked by the look-ahead so the move itself does not cause a worse blocker (the small stone, not the biggest one).
2. Find the bottleneck and the critical path (the chain of steps that sets the finish date).
3. Find hidden dependencies, missing inputs, contradictions inside the request.
4. Separate what was ASKED from what is WANTED (the goal behind the goal) and say both.
5. Mark what cannot be undone and put a gate in front of it.
6. Find the cheapest test that would settle the biggest doubt, and suggest doing it first.
**B. Looking ahead**
7. Consequence chain: next, and the step after, and the side effects on other tools and stored data.
8. Best, likely and worst case for each option, with chances.
9. Order the steps so later steps stay possible (do the reversible first, keep a way back).
10. Detect when a new fact breaks the plan and re-plan (trigger, not a schedule).
**C. Time and effort**
11. Pace against a deadline from measured past pace: "at this rate we finish on the 14th, the deadline is the 10th" with the basis.
12. Plan with spare room: the aim date is the deadline minus a margin that grows with how uncertain the estimates are (not a fixed day); say how the margin was chosen.
13. Parallel or one at a time; what can overlap safely (builder lanes, tools that touch the same file).
14. Estimate effort as a range from the journal; know when more effort stops paying (good enough).
15. Respect the owner's own limits: sessions that end, AI usage that runs out, the weakest phone. Plan so that stopping at any moment loses nothing.
16. Merge repeated steps: "do once what can be done once", propose built-in automation for anything the owner does by hand twice.
**D. Choosing**
17. Compare options on benefit, cost, risk, reversibility and effort; show the table when it matters.
18. Act, ask, or refuse: ask only when the answer would change the choice; otherwise decide and say the assumption in one line.
19. Reuse before build (FL14): does a tool already do this; does the brain do it better than the tool.
**E. Checking claims and plans**
20. Feasibility: can this really be done, with what we have, on the weakest phone; if not, what would it take.
21. Does the plan break the Charter, a law or a permission.
22. Sanity-check numbers by order of magnitude and by a second path; abstain when unsure.
23. Evidence label on every claim (PROVEN, CONFIRMED, CHECKED, REPORTED, UNSURE).
**F. Guiding the person**
24. Show the reasoning in a short visible list, not a wall of text.
25. Offer options with their consequences, in the owner's own terms and goals.
26. Notice when the owner's idea is better or when a plan has a hole, and say so with the reason (never flattery, never silence).
27. Ask the one question that lets the owner see the next step themselves; give the answer when asked for it.
28. Remember goals and how the owner likes to work (as proposals the owner approves) and use them to choose what to show first.
**G. Making things from a description (images and designs now, video later)**
29. Turn a described wish ("a poster, dark blue, the logo top left, calm") into a design BRIEF with the choices made visible (size, layout, colours, text, mood) and ask only what changes the result.
30. Produce a few variants through the chosen method, check each against the brief, and iterate on the owner's words ("less crowded", "move it left").
31. Judge which method fits (drawn from rules, traced from a reference, produced by an outside tool the owner allows); which one is a separate owner decision (OPEN_ITEMS item 16).
**H. Learning**
32. Write prediction and outcome to the journal; update pace and effort estimates; every correction becomes a test and a ledger line (roadmap 7A).

## 6. How it joins the brain (no staircase to nowhere)
- **Input:** Meaning (B03B, with the Meaning Map, references from B03B2c and B05), the live registry, the journal, the Charter, the owner's approved preferences.
- **Needs from B04A (action schemas): each tool must declare EFFECTS, PRECONDITIONS, COST and UNDO**, not only what it means. Without this the look-ahead has nothing to simulate. This is a change to B04A's contract and is proposed now so the schemas are written once.
- **Sits:** between Resolution (B04B) and Planning (B06A) in the stages of section 7, and inside the loop of 7A at "plan ONE step". Output: a verdict, the chosen plan, the alternatives left out and why, the chance and its basis, what the model could not see. The Response composer (B07) says it in AXON's voice; the chat shows the short list.
- **Order:** nothing of J starts until B04A's schema contract names effects. J0 to J3 are pure code with known-answer corpora and can be built by ChatGPT or Grok from Director contracts; J4 and J5 need the memory store (B08) and the composer.

Mutation tests in every job: break the code on purpose; the corpus must fail.

## 8. Tools before the brain: the rule (owner, 2026-10-25)
Some tools are built first ON PURPOSE: they show what the brain will have to manage, and the brain learns its abilities from them. Rule for each tool: **rate the tool today (0 to 10) and the brain's possible understanding of the same job (0 to 10).** If the brain will clearly outclass the tool (the owner's example: tool 2, brain 9) the tool is PROVISIONAL: it stays usable, its engine is not extended, and it is rebuilt on the brain after B06. If the job is exact computation (units, dates, Calculator, converters, QR, Text Counter, Roman numerals) the engine stays and the brain CALLS it (FL14); only the surface may change. First candidates: Summarizer, Text Cleaner, Pattern Tester: rebuild on the brain. Device Profile, Background Proof: audit (OPEN_ITEMS 18). The audit table is written once, not per tool.

## 9. Honest limits
- Foresight works on a MODELED situation: tasks, files, schedules, resources and AXON's own tools, and any physical scene only when the relations are given. It cannot foresee what its model lacks, and says so.
- A rule-based look-ahead is exact inside its model and silent outside it. Open-ended wisdom at a large model's level needs the optional model tier, which could only PROPOSE a model or plan that these checks then test.
- A chance is only as good as its basis; with no data it is a labelled range.
