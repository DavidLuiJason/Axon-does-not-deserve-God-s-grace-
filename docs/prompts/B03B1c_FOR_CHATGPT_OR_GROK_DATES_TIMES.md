## JOB B03B1c: DATES AND TIMES OF DAY

MINI-BASE (attached as `B03B1-MINIBASE-c.zip`; unzip it, work inside it): the mini-base of step a PLUS the accepted `values/numbers.ts` of step a (it already finds the numbers and is not yours). Files you use: `dateCalc.ts` (parseDate, formatDate, addDate, weekday, daysInMonth, isLeapYear; the ONLY source of calendar truth), `closedSets.ts` (isPreposition, isDeterminer, isCoordinator, normalizeWord), `valueWords.ts` (RELATIVE_DAY_WORDS, WEEKDAY_WORDS, MONTH_WORDS, CLOCK_WORDS, CARDINAL_WORDS; PROPOSED groups except the number words, still used here), the placeholder `values/datetimes.ts` (yours).

LANE: REPLACE `src/brain/mind/meaning/values/datetimes.ts` (it exports `readDatesTimes(ctx, found)`; keep that exact export). NEW, if you want them: `src/brain/mind/meaning/values/datetimes*.ts`; NEW `tests/valuesDatetimes.test.ts`; NEW `docs/reports/B03B1c_REPORT.md`. Nothing else.

WHAT `readDatesTimes(ctx, found)` DOES: `found` is the list of values so far (numbers). It returns the COMPLETE list: a number that becomes part of a date, a time or a datetime is REMOVED from the list and kept in the new value's `parts`; every other value passes through unchanged. The clock `ctx.clock` is local wall time GIVEN by the caller; the code never reads the real time. It never decides am or pm for the user.

THE RULES:
1. VALIDITY COMES FROM THE ENGINE. A date is valid only if `parseDate` accepts the ISO text you build; weekdays and day arithmetic come from `weekday` and `addDate`; month lengths from `daysInMonth`. No calendar table of your own.
2. DATES (`kind: 'date'`, `date` is ISO, confidence 0.9): `1 March 2027`, `March 1, 2027` (comma optional), `2027-03-01` (a year, hyphen, two-digit month, hyphen, two-digit day), numeric `13/3/2027`. A day number may be an ordinal (`1st March 2027`). `today`, `tomorrow`, `yesterday` resolve against the clock with addDate (`RELATIVE_DAY_WORDS`); a weekday name resolves to the next such day STRICTLY AFTER today (Saturday 2026-10-24: `Friday` is 2026-10-30, and `Saturday` is 2026-10-31). A weekday name directly after the article `a` or `an` is NOT a date (`is 31 February 2027 a Sunday`: a class of day, not a day). The runner proves round trips: 2,000 seeded random dates in the three written forms and the slash form.
3. NUMERIC DATES WITH SLASHES: `d/m/yyyy`. When the first number is above 12 it is the day; when the second is above 12 it is the month (US order, `3/13/2027`); when both are 12 or below and DIFFER (`1/3/2027`) the order is NOT decided: no `date`, both ISO texts in `dateAlternatives` (day-first and month-first), confidence below 0.5; when both are equal there is one date. Impossible combinations (`31/2/2027`, `13/13/2027`) are `error: 'invalid-date'`.
4. MONTH NAMES (`MONTH_WORDS`, case-insensitive, with the usual abbreviations). A month name counts ONLY next to a day number or a year: `may I convert` and `in march` are NOT dates; `may 5` and `Mar 2027 ...` follow the next rule. Day and month without a year (`5 March`): no `date`; `dateAlternatives` holds this clock year and next year; weak. Month and year without a day (`March 2027`) is not read.
5. IMPOSSIBLE DATES: `31 February 2027`, `30 February`, `29 February 2027` (not a leap year) are returned as `kind: 'date'` with `error: 'invalid-date'`, text covering the whole phrase, and no `date`. Never throw, never skip, never silently correct.
6. TIMES (`kind: 'time'`, `times` is a list of `HH:MM` 24-hour candidates): `7:30am` (07:30), `8pm` (20:00), `12am` (00:00), `12pm` (12:00), `20:15`, `noon` (12:00), `midnight` (00:00), `half past eight`, `quarter to nine` (08:45), `quarter past 3`, and `<number> past <hour>` (`ten past five`, 05:10). Words for the hour come from step a's numbers or the number words. NOT supported on purpose: `<number> to <hour>` (it collides with ranges like `5 to 9`). With am or pm stated (attached or after one space) there is ONE candidate; hour above 12 with am or pm (`13pm`) is `error: 'invalid-time'`. `H:MM` or `HH:MM` (a one- or two-digit hour, a two-digit minute) is a time when the hour is 0 to 23 and the minute 0 to 59 (otherwise `error: 'invalid-time'` only if it follows `at`; else leave the numbers alone). Without am or pm: an hour written WITH A LEADING ZERO (`07:30`, `00:15`), or 0, or 13 to 23 is plainly 24-hour and has ONE candidate (`20:15`, `07:30` is 07:30); an hour of 1 to 12 written without a leading zero (`7:30`, `10:30`, `12:30`) has BOTH candidates (`7:30` gives 07:30 and 19:30; `12:30` gives 00:30 and 12:30). The same two-candidate rule holds for the spoken forms (`half past eight` gives 08:30 and 20:30; `quarter to nine` 08:45 and 20:45; `at 12` gives 00:00 and 12:00). A BARE hour after the preposition `at` (use `isPreposition`; `at 8`) is a time with both candidates and its text INCLUDES the word `at` (`at 8` gives 08:00 and 20:00); a time written with minutes or am or pm does NOT include the `at` in its text (`meet at 20:15` has the time text `20:15`). A bare number without `at` (`an alarm for 8 tomorrow`) is NOT a time: the number 8 stays a number. Confidence 0.9 when one candidate, 0.7 when two. The code never chooses am or pm.
7. DATETIME (`kind: 'datetime'`, `date` and `times` both set, `parts: [date, time]`): a date and a time next to each other in one phrase, in either order, with only whitespace, a comma, or the words `at` or `on` between (`tomorrow at 8`, `8pm tomorrow`, `7:30am on Friday`). The `at` between them belongs to the phrase.
8. NUMBERS inside a date or time move into `parts`. Numbers that are not part of any date or time pass through unchanged (including the 8 in `alarm for 8 tomorrow`). Words that merely contain month or weekday names (`mayor`, `marching`, `decent`) are never read: whole words only. Never cross a range edge.
