WHAT `readNumbers` DOES: it returns every NUMBER in the language ranges, as `kind: 'number'` values, sorted by start, not overlapping. It returns numbers ONLY (a later step turns a number plus a unit into a quantity, percent or duration; another turns numbers into dates and times). So it must read a number the same way wherever it sits, and a digit run that is part of something bigger (the 5 in `5 miles`, the 7 and 30 in `7:30`) is still returned as its own number; the later steps merge them.

THE RULES (derived from the owner's real messages; each corpus item of job a shows one):
1. DIGITS. A run of digits is a number (`12345`, `4521`). An optional sign directly attached: a minus counts as a sign ONLY at the start of the text or right after a space, so `-5` is minus five but `5-3` is two numbers 5 and 3, and in `2027-03-01` the parts are 2027, 03 and 01 (leading zeros are fine: value 3 and 1; keep the original text). Decimals with a point (`3.5`, a point not followed by a digit ends the number). Thousands commas only in exact groups of three after one to three digits (`1,000`, `12,345,678`); any other comma separates numbers. A digit run directly preceded by a letter (`mp3`, `B03B1`, and the `30` in `1h30m`) is still returned, but WEAK (confidence 0.3) with `alternatives: [{ kind: 'identifier', note: <why> }]`; a later step merges attached pairs such as `1h30m`. `form: 'digits'`, confidence 0.95 otherwise.
2. WORDS (form `words`, confidence 0.9). Only words in CARDINAL_WORDS (zero to nineteen, twenty to ninety, hundred, thousand, million) are number words, matched as WHOLE words, case-insensitive (`tennis`, `won`, `for`, `to`, `tenth` are never read as ten, one, four, two: only the whole word counts). Compounds with a hyphen or a space: `twenty-five`, `twenty five` (25), `two thousand five hundred` (2500), `three million` (3000000), `one hundred and twenty` (120). Read the grammar of English counting, not a list of examples: a number phrase is a sequence of groups, each group below a thousand being [units `hundred`] [`and`] [tens] [units]; the groups are multiplied by `thousand` or `million` and added. A wrong order ends the number (`twenty twenty` is two numbers, 20 and 20; `two three` is 2 and 3; `five twenty` is 5 and 20). The joiner `and` (use `isCoordinator`) belongs to the number only between a `hundred` or a scale word and the number words after it (`one hundred and twenty`); in `twenty five and 3` the `and` is NOT part of the number. `a hundred`, `a thousand`, `a million` (the article comes from `isDeterminer`; text includes the `a`) are 100, 1000, 1000000. A scale word alone (`hundreds of people`, a bare `hundred`) is not read. `two and a half` is one number, 2.5 (`and a half` after a number adds 0.5; text includes it). A bare `half` is the number 0.5 with confidence 0.6 and its text is just `half`, never the article before it (a later step turns `half an hour` and `an hour and a half` into durations). Do NOT read `minus` or `negative` as signs: that word group is not approved yet.
3. THE WORD `one` IS ALSO A PRONOUN (`the last one`, `which one`). When `one` stands ALONE as the whole number phrase, return value 1 with confidence 0.4 (weak) and `alternatives: [{ kind: 'pronoun', note: <why> }]`. When `one` is part of a longer number phrase (`one hundred`, `twenty one`, `one thousand`) it is strong like any other. (A later step raises `one mile` to a strong quantity.)
4. ORDINALS. Words `first` to `tenth` (ORDINAL_WORDS): value 1 to 10, `form: 'ordinal'`, confidence 0.9. `second` is an ordinal by default (`the second one` gives the ordinal 2 and then the weak `one`). Digits with a suffix: `2nd`, `21st`, `3rd`, `11th`, `112th`, `101st`; the suffix must AGREE with the number by the English rule (11, 12, 13 and every number ending in 11, 12, 13 take `th`; otherwise 1 takes `st`, 2 `nd`, 3 `rd`, the rest `th`); a wrong suffix (`1th`, `2st`, `13rd`) is returned as `error: 'bad-ordinal'` with no value, text covering the digits and the suffix. Suffix case does not matter. The ordinal's text is the whole thing (`2nd`, `21st`).
5. IDENTIFIERS. A digit string with a leading zero and more than 3 digits, or with 9 or more digits, still gets its value but is WEAK (confidence 0.3) with `alternatives: [{ kind: 'identifier', note: <why> }]` (`08031234567` is a phone number, not 8 billion).
6. TOO LARGE. A value beyond the safe integer range is `error: 'too-large'` (no value); a decimal that cannot be represented the same way. Never throw.
7. Never read a number inside material (quoted text, code): the ranges already exclude it; never cross a range edge.
