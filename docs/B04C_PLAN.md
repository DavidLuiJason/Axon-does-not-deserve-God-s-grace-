# B04C PLAN: typed execution (Director, 2026-10-28)
Roadmap 7E, 7I and the ledger rows C0a to C2g. This file holds the evidence and the split; the board keeps the story.

## What B04C is
Today `execution` in `src/brain/mind/stages.ts` hands the RAW TEXT to the legacy executor (regex handlers in `localRules.ts`, `textCountCommand.ts`). B04C makes a matched tool run from chat with TYPED arguments only (values found by Meaning, text taken from quoted material or from a clause detail span, never from the whole message), one family per job, each retiring a legacy handler. Only 3 of 63 capabilities have a chat action today (text-counter, dictionary, taught-meanings); 58 are `not-built` in chat.

## Evidence (MEASURED 2026-10-28, `tests/toolRequestAccuracy.test.ts`)
Does Resolution reach the RIGHT tool for a clear request? 57 requests, 28 tools in the five B04C families.
- **Dev corpus** (`tests/fixtures/toolRequests.json`, builders may read it): exact 21/57 (37%); asked with the right choice among others 12; wrong tool 12; nothing 12. Per family: text 7/16, numbers 0/6, time 4/8, create 7/10, files and converter 3/17.
- **Held-out corpus** (different wording, kept OUTSIDE the repository by the owner): exact 11/57 (19%).
- Examples: "Count the words in 'the quick brown fox'" resolves to the translator; "Convert 5 miles to kilometres" asks among audio, case, document and file converters; "What is 15 percent of 240" and "How many days until 2026-12-25" find nothing.
- Reading: a clause with no action verb (a question like "What is 15 percent of 240") has little to match, and typed values (percent, date, quantity with unit) are not used as evidence for a tool. So B04C cannot start with the runners: a runner behind the wrong match answers the wrong question.

## C0b-D DESIGN (Director, 2026-10-28; DONE; the contract is in the code, this is the reasoning)
**Finding (MEASURED, scratch run of the real stages on the 57 dev requests).** Of the 12 "nothing found" requests, 6 carry a strong value and have no concept key to match ("What is 15 percent of 240", "How many days until 2026-12-25", "How many pounds is 3 kilograms", "Count down from 5 minutes"); the other 6 carry no such value. The values stage DOES find what they carry (percent, date, duration, quantity); Resolution only uses values after the match, to list missing slots. The adapter also folds a percent into a plain number.
**Design.** Four additions, all optional so nothing existing changes: (1) `ProvidedSlot.value` keeps the kind (percent stays a number slot AND is evidence); (2) `ActionSchema.values` lists the PRIMARY value kinds a tool is for (0 to 2 of `VALUE_KIND_LABELS`: percent, quantity, duration, date, time); (3) a third key group `v` in the scoring, weighed like any key, matched by identity, times `VALUE_WEIGHT` = 1 (rules 8, 9, 11 in `resolveTypes.ts`); (4) `Common.evidence` says whether the winner rested on concept, value or both (rule 10). Why a new declaration and not "read the slots" as first planned: MEASURED, percent has no slot kind, and the converter declares no quantity slot, so slots alone cannot say what a tool is for.
**Never evidence:** a bare number, a Roman numeral, quoted text. MEASURED: the reader finds the number 64 inside "Base64", and "two files" and "one go" read as numbers. **Trap:** "Is 5 minutes a long time" carries a duration but is not a timer request: a value-only winner is allowed to match, and C1 (execution) decides whether to confirm before running.
**Prototype (scratch only, not in the repository).** Declaring percent for the percentage helper, quantity for the converter, duration for the timer and date for the date calculator: dev exact 21 to 27 of 57, nothing found 12 to 6, wrong tool 12 to 11, asked-with-the-right-choice 12 to 13. A wider declaration set (also split-bill percent, loan duration, date calculator duration) gave only 25, because tools tie and Resolution asks: declare PRIMARY kinds only. The held-out corpus was NOT run on the prototype (the owner keeps it away): C0b-C is scored on it by the Director.
**What it does not fix.** 30 of the 57 requests still miss after the prototype, mostly text and file requests with no typed value (vocabulary: C0c-1 to C0c-3); the targets (85% dev) are far away. Arithmetic ("What is 12 times 7 plus 3") has no value kind: the reader does not read expressions (OPEN_ITEMS 50). Three reader gaps are recorded in `tests/fixtures/valueCases.json` (temperature in degrees, "5 years", "24 hour time"; OPEN_ITEMS 49).
**C0b-C (builder: ChatGPT or Grok), the scope.** Allowed files: `src/brain/mind/schema/actionSchemas.ts` (read an optional last column `values` in the DATA table; declare it for exactly four tools to start: percentage-helper, converter, timer, date-calculator), `src/brain/mind/resolve/resolve.ts` (rules 8 to 11), `src/brain/mind/resolve/clauseInputs.ts` (fill `ProvidedSlot.value`), new tests. Never the contract files, `package.json`, the corpora or the baseline. Proof: dev exact at least 27/57, wrong tool not above 11, `brainGolden` 79 messages unchanged, `valueCases` and `toolRequestAccuracy` pass, a MUTANT test (a bare number gives no key). The Director then runs the held-out corpus and raises the ratchet.

## C0c-1 RESULT AND RE-PLAN (Director, 2026-10-28; MEASURED on the real stack)
**What was done, and why the Director did it and not a builder.** A builder has no dictionary in its mini-base, so it cannot measure a vocabulary change; the Director edited the 13 text-writing and create-encode rows of `actionSchemas.ts` and measured. RULE used (so nothing is tuned to a sentence): a schema's words come from the tool's OWN menu description and purposes in `toolFacts.json`; no light verbs (say, make, turn, give, show: they collide with every other tool); no word that makes another tool lose its own concept (the schema runner says so); a name must not be a dictionary word (`hex` was refused, `hexadecimal:a` is an object instead).
**Result.** Dev exact 27 to 31 of 57 (create-encode 7/10 to 10/10; text-writing 7/16 to 8/16; wrong tool 11 to 10). A SECOND dev corpus in fresh wording (`tests/fixtures/toolRequestsDev2.json`, 26 requests for the same 13 tools, written after the first was tuned) went only 7 to 9 of 26. So most of the dev gain does not carry over: vocabulary is NOT the main lever. The accuracy test now ratchets both corpora (31/57 and 9/26).
**Why the rest fails (MEASURED, 18 misses on dev2; my grouping, approximate).** (A) the speech act: a statement of need ("I need a QR code for this link", "I need a new password") is read as chat, so it is not a request: 3. (B) the verb or clause is read wrongly: a plural noun taken as the verb ("How many words are in my essay"), a question word as the verb, a clause split, "wrong" read as negation: about 5. (C) closeness without sense: "sound" reaches the audio converter, "note" reaches the bill splitter, "pick" and "clean", a verb shared by several tools (choose, check, search), or two tools tied on one shared word: about 6. (D) genuine vocabulary gaps ("capital letters", "flip a coin", "title case"): about 4. Only D is vocabulary.
**New rows (Director designs, builders build).** C0d-A: the Meaning stage reads a statement of need as a request (design first, then a builder). C0d-B: choosing the verb and clause for question forms and plural nouns. C0d-C: sense-aware closeness in Resolution (use the B03B4 senses so "pick" is not "clean"; a closeness may not decide alone). C0c-2 and C0c-3 stay, as small Director vocabulary jobs by the same rule, expected to gain little on their own. The targets (85% dev, 70% held-out) cannot be reached by C0c; they need C0d-A to C0d-C.

## C0d-A DESIGN (Director, 2026-10-28; MEASURED on the real stack; builds on the owner's yes to OPEN_ITEMS 54)
**Decision.** "I need / I want / I would like (or I'd like) + a thing a tool makes" is read as a REQUEST. Owner: "go ahead with what you think is right"; Director: yes.
**Finding (MEASURED).** Today all of these are `chat` (0.8, "a subject with a finite verb") and Resolution answers `not-a-request`. Only 2 of the 83 corpus requests are statements of need (both in dev2), so the corpus can show at most +2; the real reason is how people talk. A scratch prototype (diff kept in `docs/director/B04C0dA_prototype.diff.txt`, NOT code): dev 31/57 unchanged, dev2 9/26 to 11/26, golden 79 unchanged. It reads "I need a QR code", "I need a new password", "I want a random number", "We need a timer", "I want a hash" correctly. It also exposed four defects the design must handle: (1) "I'd like..." stays chat (the contraction is one unit); (2) "I needed a password yesterday" wrongly fires (the past tense carries the same dictionary sense); (3) "I need a break" and similar statements must not reach a tool by accident (rule 13; "I need help" still reaches the unavailable AI assistant, kept on purpose as an honest answer, OPEN_ITEMS 58); (4) "I need to count the words" lost the real verb (parts.verb is "need"; "to count the words in this text" sits in a detail span) and asked among four tools.
**Design, in the order the stages run (no phrase lists, FL13).**
1. **Dictionary senses, one file** `meaning/structure/needSenses.ts`, same pattern as `speechSenses.ts`: NEED = 01191258 (have need of), 01191646 (have or feel a need for), 01829179 (feel or have a desire for); WOULD-LIKE = 01828678 (to hope, desire or prefer to have), 01829904 (want to have). The last two count ONLY after `would` or the contraction `'d`, because the same senses make "I like pizza" a want. Left out on purpose: 01781131 (find enjoyable: "I like pizza"), `require`, `wish` (hypothetical), 00711034 (demand the presence of). Lex gets two flags (`need`, `wouldLike`) and `baseForm` (the token's lemma equals the word, so "needed" fails).
2. **Meaning (`acts.ts`)**: a new reading `command` with `need: true`, confidence 0.82 (above chat 0.8, so chat stays as the alternative), ONLY when: first-person subject (`speaker`), `would` allowed between subject and verb, a NEED verb in base form (or WOULD-LIKE after would), clause positive, not a question, no condition link, a complement follows, and the complement does not start with an object pronoun ("I need it", "I want you to" stay chat). `ClauseFrame.need` carries it.
3. **Adapter (`clauseInputs.ts`)**: contract rule 11 in `clauseInputsTypes.ts`: the need verb is not a verb key; the verb of the wanted action comes from the `to` detail span ("to count"); a thing ("a QR code") has no verb.
4. **Resolution (`resolve.ts`)**: contract rules 12 to 14 in `resolveTypes.ts`: scored exactly like a command, but only `match` or `unavailable` may come out; `ask`, `none` and `negated` become `not-a-request`. A statement is not an order, so AXON never asks "which tool?" about "I need a break".
5. **Running it is C1's decision (OPEN_ITEMS 55):** a need statement is weaker evidence than an order, so the execution contract should confirm once before a tool with an effect runs ("Make a QR code for this link?"); a free, undoable tool may run at once.
**Proof.** `tests/fixtures/needCases.json`: 14 need statements that must give the SAME result as their plain command (a differential test: nothing is tuned to a sentence; three plain forms miss today, so only equality is demanded there) and 14 statements that must stay conversation. Builder acceptance (C0d-A-C): that runner, dev at least 31/57, dev2 at least 11/26, wrong tool not above 10, golden 79 unchanged, five mutants caught (a past tense fires; a negation fires; `like` without `would` fires; an `ask` leaks out of a need clause; the need verb becomes a verb key).
**What it does not fix.** "I want you to count the words" (a request to AXON itself), "I am looking for a QR code maker" (progressive verb), "I need it by Friday" (needs a reference). Recorded as OPEN_ITEMS 56 and 57. It also does not fix the verb choice behind "Count the words in this text" reaching the translator: that is C0d-B (the need form can only be as good as its plain form).
**RESULT (Director built it itself, same turn; MEASURED on the real dictionary).** The reference proved small and safe (about 40 lines in 6 files), so no builder prompt was written: a second hand-off would only have added risk. Files: new `meaning/structure/needSenses.ts`; `lexical.ts` (flags `need`, `wouldLike`, `baseForm`, `wouldClitic`); `acts.ts`; `build.ts`; `resolve/clauseInputs.ts`; `resolve/resolve.ts` (a wrapper, rule 13). Two defects found while building: the dictionary lists "needed" as its own adjective, so past tense is told by the regular "-ed" form of the alternative lemma (every need or want verb is regular); and "need" has a stray alternative lemma "nee", so only the "-ed" rule is used. Results: dev 31/57 (unchanged), dev2 9 to 11 of 26, HELD-OUT 17 to 18 of 57 (the one need statement in it, "I need a QR code for my website link"), golden 79 messages unchanged, needCases 76 checks (14 need statements equal to their plain commands, 13 that stay conversation), clauseInputs 277, resolve 1574, valueEvidence 110, capabilityTruth 236, tsc clean, guard OK. Mutants: past tense, `like` without `would`, and an `ask` leaking out of a need clause are CAUGHT; a negation and the need verb as a verb key SURVIVE as equivalent mutants (the structure already blocks both; recorded, not claimed as caught). Also fixed: `tests/actionSchemasRunner.test.ts` in the repository failed on the four tools that declare `values` (the key check did not allow the optional field); it now passes (487 checks, 44 mutants caught).
**HELD-OUT SCORE (owner file, MEASURED 2026-10-28): 17/57 exact before C0d-A, 18/57 after** (was 11/57 at C0a). Per family before: text 2/16, numbers 1/6, time 4/8, create 7/10, files 3/17. The dev corpus is 31/57 and the text family is 8/16 on dev but 2/16 held-out: part of the dev gain is wording tuned to the dev sentences. The honest target is the held-out number.
